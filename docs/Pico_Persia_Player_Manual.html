PicoComputer 3 · player's manual

Pico Persia

A dungeon game for the PC3, in the style of the Apple II original. The program is here; the game's pictures, rooms and animation are not. You supply those yourself from a copy you obtain, and a converter turns them into the form the PC3 reads.

Read this first

Nothing of the game is included here. This package holds an engine and a converter, both written from scratch. It contains no artwork, no rooms, no animation and no sound. Until you have run the converter over your own copy of the published source release, it will not start.

That is deliberate. The source release is published but grants no rights to the game's content, so the content is not ours to pass on. It stays yours, on your machine.

What you need

  • A PicoComputer 3 running recent MMBasic, with a keyboard and a display. Sound needs an audio output configured; without one the game runs silently rather than refusing to start.
  • Python 3.8 or later on a PC, to run the converter once.
  • Your own copy of the published Apple II source release, obtained by you. The converter never downloads anything.

Setting it up

  1. Get the source release

    Clone or download the published Apple II source release yourself, to a folder on your PC. The converter needs the folder that contains the Images and Levels directories; it will find them whether you point it at the top of your copy or at the source folder inside it.

  2. Convert the assets

    python convert.py --source <your copy> --out <output folder>

    This reads your copy and writes a set of data files onto your machine. It takes a few seconds. convert.log records what it read and produced, which is worth keeping if anything looks wrong later.

    By default it converts what the game itself needs and packs the artwork into four image sheets. Add --set full to convert the introduction and ending artwork as well, which needs a fifth sheet and leaves no room on the board for anything else.

    FileWhat it is
    sheet1.bmp …all the artwork, already in the display's colour format
    art.binwhere every picture sits in which sheet
    levels.datthe fifteen levels
    frames.dat, seq.datthe animation: every pose, and the sequences that order them
    blocks.dat, tables.idxhow a room is built, and where everything else is
    sounds.datthe twenty sound effects, as a pitch and a length each
    cutroom.bmpthe princess's room, for the scenes between levels, if your copy has it

  3. Build the program

    python build.py player.bas -o player.min.bas

    The engine is written to be read, and nearly a third of it is explanation. MMBasic keeps the whole text in memory, so the comments have to come out before it will fit. This removes them and nothing else.

  4. Copy everything to the board

    Put the converted data files and the program in the same folder on the board's drive. Copy player.min.bas across as player.bas.

    Any method will do. On a board with networking the fastest is TFTP; otherwise use the SD card, or send the files down the console.

  5. Run it

    RUN "player.bas"

    It reports what it found as it loads, including whether sound is available. If a file is missing it will say so.

Controls

Everything is done with the four arrow keys and one action key.

The action key

Either shift key. The space bar does exactly the same, for keyboards that will not report shift. Wherever the tables below say action, that is the key they mean.

KeyWhat it does
Shift or SpaceThe action key, written as action below. On its own it does nothing; it changes what the other keys do.
Left, RightRun. Tapped from standing he starts to run; held, he keeps going.
UpJump. Standing under a ledge he jumps and catches it; with a run-up it becomes a running jump. At an open exit he climbs the stairs.
DownCrouch. From a crouch, press a direction to crawl.
Shift + directionA careful step, exactly as far as the edge in front of him. Press again at the edge and he tests the ground with a foot; again and he steps off.
Shift while fallingReach for a ledge. Hold it as you go over an edge and he will catch what he can.
Shift while hangingHold on. Let go and he drops. Press up instead and he climbs.
Shift, standing stillTake what is in front of him. He crouches first; press again and he picks it up.
EscapeStop playing and return to the prompt.

With a sword

He draws it by himself when an enemy comes into view, and puts it away when the danger has passed.

KeyWhat it does
Shift or SpaceStrike.
UpBlock. Timed against his blade it turns the blow aside.
Forward, BackAdvance and retreat.
DownLower the sword and put it away.

Playing

You have sixty minutes to get through the dungeon. The clock runs whenever you are alive, and speaks up as it passes each mark. Run out and the game is over wherever you stand.

The bar at the bottom left is your strength. Each square is one blow or one bad landing. The bar at the bottom right belongs to whoever you are fighting. Lose your last square and you die, and the level starts again.

Things worth knowing

  • A fall of one storey hurts, two kills. Catching a ledge on the way down costs nothing, so reach for one whenever you go over an edge.
  • Loose floors give way a moment after you stand on them, and take whatever is underneath with them.
  • Pressure plates raise gates, sometimes a room away. A plate stays down only briefly, so a gate you opened will close again.
  • Spikes spring when you walk over them. Walking carefully over sprung spikes is safe; running or landing on them is not.
  • Potions do different things. Most restore you, one makes you stronger for good, one turns the world upside down and one is poison. There is no way to tell which is which except by drinking it.
  • The guards will not let you past. Unarmed you have no chance at all, so find the sword before you meet one.
  • Anything you can pick up is coloured. A potion bottle is green and the sword is gold. In the original they are drawn in the same stone colours as the wall behind them, which on this display made them almost impossible to see, so they are given colours of their own. Nothing else in the game uses either one.

Optional extras

A title screen

Put a title.bmp beside the data files and it is shown until you press a key. Leave it out and the game starts straight away. A 640 by 480 picture fits the screen exactly.

The scenes between levels

The game cuts away to the princess's room on the way into levels 2, 4, 6, 8, 9 and 12, and at both endings. The picture is held for a few seconds and any key moves you on.

The converter finds the picture and writes cutroom.bmp without being asked, so there is nothing to do. If your copy does not have it the scenes are skipped and the game plays exactly as before.

Music

The game asks for sixteen tunes by name. None are supplied and none are needed: with no music present every cue is simply ignored. If you want music, obtain your own copies and put them in a music folder beside the data as WAV files named after the cue: danger, sword, potion, stairs, upstairs, victory and tragic are the ones the game currently reaches.

The files must be PCM WAV, mono or stereo, eight or sixteen bit, up to 48 kHz. The board plays one thing at a time, so a sound effect is dropped while a tune is playing rather than cutting the tune short.

If something goes wrong

What you seeWhat it means
It will not start, and names a fileThe converter has not been run, or not everything was copied across. Check the list in step two.
No soundThe board has no audio output configured. The game says so as it loads and then runs silently.
Not enough memoryThe program was copied across with its comments still in it. Run the build step and copy the stripped file.
Destination array too smallThe data files and the program are from different versions. Convert and build again from the same copy.
The colours look wrongExpected in a saved screenshot, which does not carry the game's palette. On the display itself they should be right.
