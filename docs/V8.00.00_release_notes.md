# PicoMite V8.00.00 release notes

**Beta 4 (V8.00.00b4).** V8 starts from V7.00.00 and adds three things. On
the PicoMitePC3, `RUN "name.app"` runs a program written in C or C++ at full
speed from the PSRAM, with the firmware's files, display, keyboard,
gamepads, mouse, sound, I/O and network as services, and `USER LOAD` adds
commands and functions of your own to MMBasic, also written in C (see *New
in V8: loadable programs on the PicoMitePC3* and *New in V8: USER modules*).
On the VGA and HDMI builds, `FRAMEBUFFER WARPH` and `FRAMEBUFFER WARPV` copy
a framebuffer with every row or column moved by its own offset, for the
raster effects of the old arcade games: a racing road with curves and hills,
parallax scrolling, rippling water (see *New in V8: FRAMEBUFFER WARPH and
WARPV*). And on every display a shape that runs off the edge of the screen
no longer leaves a line of pixels along that edge (see *Fixed in V8*). Five
apps already run on the PicoComputer 3 with it: the Colour Maximite 2's NES
emulator, Doom, MicroPython, and, new in beta 2, the BBC Micro and ScummVM
(see *The first apps*). Beta 2 brought loadable programs to the
PicoMiteHDMIUSB, `LOOP`, `CHANGE`, `STOP` and a pan to `PLAY MODSAMPLE` for
continuous sound effects, 720x576 to the HDMI builds, and CPU speeds up to
444 MHz to the RP2350 (see *New in V8.00.00b2*). Beta 3 adds SQL databases
to the PicoMitePC3 and the PicoMiteHDMIUSB, and `FRAMEBUFFER AFFINE` and
`SPAN` to the VGA and HDMI builds, for a picture turned and zoomed, a floor
in perspective and windows of any shape (see *New in V8.00.00b3*); it fixes
`FORMAT$`, `BLIT RESIZE`, and a web server program that skipped its own
errors (see *Fixed in V8.00.00b3*). Beta 4 adds threads to every build: a
SUB started with `THREAD START` runs alongside the main program, with its
own loops, local variables and `PAUSE`s, and the firmware shares the
processor between them (see *New in V8.00.00b4: threads*). In a program a
listing that pauses at every screen full is now refused, and `LIST
VARIABLES`, `LIST COMMANDS`, `LIST FUNCTIONS` and `LIST PINS` have `ALL`
forms that do not pause (see *Changed in V8.00.00b4*). Beta 4 also fixes the
RTC, `WEB TRANSMIT FILE`, a TLS connection that the server closed, `LIBRARY
LOAD` and empty RAM and flash slots (see *Fixed in V8.00.00b4*). More
services and apps will follow in later betas. Coming from V7.00.00 two
builds reset at their first boot, from beta 2 programs saved by an earlier
firmware must be loaded again, coming from beta 2 three builds reset at
their first boot of beta 3, and coming from beta 3 every build resets at its
first boot of beta 4 (see the boxes below). `MM.VER` reads `8.000004`.

> **Coming from V7.00.00, two builds reset at their first boot**: the
> PicoMitePC3 has moved its flash region up 16 KB to make room for the
> loader, and the PicoMiteRP2350VGAUSB to make room for `FRAMEBUFFER WARPH`
> and `WARPV`. Their options return to their defaults (on the PicoMitePC3
> with them its Bluetooth pairings, so pair your keyboards and speakers
> again; a PicoComputer 3 configures itself again, but give it its WiFi
> network again), and their flash slots and program memory are cleared, so
> save the options first (`OPTION DISK SAVE`) and load them again afterwards
> (`OPTION DISK LOAD`). On a board with 4 MB of flash, such as a Pico 2, it
> is a full reset, A: drive included; on a board with 16 MB, such as the
> PicoComputer 3, the A: drive is kept. Every other build keeps everything:
> its options, its programs, its flash slots and its A: drive.

> **From V8.00.00b2, on every build, programs saved by an earlier firmware
> must be loaded again**: to free function tokens for new functions, `EOF(`,
> `LOC(` and `LOF(` now share one token and `RAY(` is part of `SPRITE(`, so
> most functions have a new token number. A program tokenised by V7.00.00 or
> V8.00.00b1 lists and runs with the wrong functions: the program in memory,
> the flash and RAM slots, a library made with `LIBRARY SAVE`, and a `.lib`
> file made with `LIBRARY DISK SAVE`. Load each again from its `.bas` file
> (`LOAD`, then `FLASH SAVE n` or `LIBRARY SAVE`), and make the `.lib` files
> again; the `.bas` files themselves are text and are not affected. If
> `OPTION AUTORUN` runs a program at startup, turn it off before updating
> (`OPTION AUTORUN OFF`) and on again once the program is loaded. A library
> that a program puts in with `LIBRARY LOAD` is loaded again by itself the
> first time the program runs: it asks first, as it does for any other
> library, unless the `O` option is given. The PicoMiteHDMIUSB also resets
> at its first boot of V8.00.00b2, as the box above describes: it now runs
> loadable programs too, and its flash region has moved up 16 KB to make
> room for the loader.

> **Coming from V8.00.00b2, three builds reset at their first boot of
> V8.00.00b3**: their flash regions have moved up 16 KB more, the
> PicoMitePC3's (to 1792 KB) to make room for SQL, and the PicoMiteHDMIUSB's
> and the PicoMiteVGAUSB's (RP2040) to make room for `FRAMEBUFFER AFFINE` and
> `SPAN`. As in the first box, their options return to their defaults (save
> them first with `OPTION DISK SAVE` and load them again with
> `OPTION DISK LOAD`), and their flash slots and program memory are cleared.
> On a board with 2 or 4 MB of flash, such as a Pico or a Pico 2, it is a
> full reset, A: drive included; on a board with 16 MB, such as the
> PicoComputer 3, the A: drive is kept. Every other build keeps everything.
> Programs saved by beta 2 need not be loaded again: `SQL(` is a new
> function token after all the others, so no other token has moved.

> **Coming from V8.00.00b3, every build resets at its first boot of
> V8.00.00b4**: every build's flash region has moved up 16 KB to make room
> for threads (the WebMiteRP2350's 32 KB, as the RTC fix needed room too).
> As in the first box, the options return to their defaults (save them first
> with `OPTION DISK SAVE` and load them again with `OPTION DISK LOAD`), and
> the flash slots and program memory are cleared. On a board with 2 or 4 MB
> of flash, such as a Pico or a Pico 2, it is a full reset, A: drive
> included; on a board with 16 MB, such as the PicoComputer 3, the A: drive
> is kept. The PicoMiteRP2040MIN keeps its A: drive too: its program memory
> is 4 KB smaller instead (108 KB). Programs saved by beta 3 need not be
> loaded again: `THREAD` is a new command after all the others, so no token
> has moved.

## New in V8.00.00b2

- **Loadable programs on the PicoMiteHDMIUSB**, once its PSRAM is enabled
  (see *New in V8: loadable programs on the PicoMitePC3*). Its flash region
  has moved to make room for the loader, so it resets at its first boot of
  beta 2 (see the box above).
- **`PLAY MODSAMPLE`: `LOOP`, `CHANGE`, `STOP`, a rate and a pan**, for
  continuous sound effects such as an engine that follows its revs or a car
  going by from one speaker to the other (see *New in V8.00.00b2: PLAY
  MODSAMPLE for continuous sounds*).
- **720x576 (576p) on the HDMI builds** (not the PicoMiteHDMIWEB or
  PicoMiteHDMIBTH): `OPTION RESOLUTION 720x576`, at 50 frames a second with
  the CPU at 404 MHz. MODE 1 and 3 are 720 x 576 and MODE 2, 4 and 5 are
  360 x 288; the console is 90 x 48 characters. Its framebuffer is larger
  than at 640x480, so it takes more of the heap: on the PicoComputer 3 the
  SRAM heap is 55.5 KB at this resolution, not 108 KB.
- **CPU speeds up to 444 MHz on the RP2350.** `OPTION CPUSPEED` and
  `CPU SPEED` accept up to 444 MHz (from 48 MHz on the PicoMite builds,
  126 MHz on the WebMite and 200 MHz on the Bluetooth builds), with or
  without PSRAM: the 384 MHz limit with PSRAM has gone. The VGA and HDMI
  builds run at up to 404 MHz. A PicoComputer 3 passed every test at
  456 MHz.
- **Flash writes at any CPU speed (RP2350).** The firmware now writes its
  flash itself (the A: drive, the options, `SAVE`, the library and the flash
  slots), at a speed of its own whatever the CPU clock, and sets the flash's
  read timing from the clock. Overclocked, these used to be the first things
  to fail.
- **Loadable programs can have an SRAM part**: code and data that the loader
  copies into the SRAM for the run, for what is too slow from the PSRAM
  (`build.py --sram`, see `tools/sdk/README.md`). The BBC Micro's 6502 runs
  from it.
- **Three function tokens freed**: `EOF(`, `LOC(` and `LOF(` share one token
  and `RAY(` is part of `SPRITE(`, so programs saved by an earlier firmware
  must be loaded again (see the box above). `LIBRARY LOAD` loads such a
  library again by itself.

## New in V8: loadable programs on the PicoMitePC3

A loadable program is C or C++ built separately from the firmware, with the
SDK in the firmware's `tools/sdk` folder (the Arm GNU Toolchain 13.3 and
Python 3), and run with

    RUN "name.app" [, args$]

It runs from the PSRAM at full speed and returns to MMBasic when it ends.
The firmware lends it its services through a table:

- the console, the time, the files and directories on A:, B: and C:;
- the display, its modes and its palette;
- the keyboard, gamepads and the mouse;
- sound: a 16-bit stereo stream at 44.1 kHz through `OPTION AUDIO`'s output;
- I/O: pins, ADC, PWM, I2C, SPI and the COM ports, on the pins BASIC gave
  them;
- setting the clock, the watchdog, and the network: Wi-Fi, DNS, TCP and UDP;
- the firmware's double-precision maths, which the RP2350's coprocessor
  speeds up.

The services have the Colour Maximite 2's names and meanings, so the
portable parts of a program build for either. Copy the `.app` file to the
board by TFTP or another transfer that keeps its exact size: XMODEM pads a
file, and the loader then refuses it. A program must call the poll service
often (each frame of a game), as that is when the USB keyboard, the network
and Ctrl-C are looked after. A fault in a program restarts the board.

`tools/sdk/README.md` describes building and writing a program, and its
`examples` folder has one for each group of services. The design is in
`docs/Loader_Plan.md`.

From V8.00.00b2 the PicoMiteHDMIUSB runs them too, once its PSRAM is
enabled (`OPTION PSRAM PIN`). Without PSRAM, `RUN` says that loadable
programs need it, and a firmware without the loader says that it cannot run
them, where it used to try to load the file as a BASIC program.

### The first apps

Five programs from the Colour Maximite 2's apps already run on the
PicoComputer 3. They are in `PicoMite_Programs-8.00.00b4.zip`, posted with
the firmware, as the Colour Maximite 2's are: copy its folders to the root
of the SD card. Its `README.txt` tells you how to start, use and leave each
one, and gives their licences and where their source is. From beta 3 it also
has the `sql` folder that the SQL command needs (see *New in V8.00.00b3: SQL
databases*).

- **NES**: the Colour Maximite 2's NES emulator (frank-nes, on the QuickNES
  core), in MODE 5 at about 55 frames a second at 315 MHz, with the USB
  keyboard and gamepads, and two free games, Nova the Squirrel and Thwaite.
- **Doom**: with Freedoom, a free replacement for Doom's game data, at 28
  frames a second in its timedemo. It has its sound effects but no music,
  as the PicoMite has no FM synthesiser.
- **MicroPython**: MicroPython 1.29 of the PicoComputer 3, with its display,
  keyboard, mouse, sound, files, pins, I2C, SPI, PWM, the RTC and watchdog,
  and the network (sockets, TLS, `requests`, MQTT and NTP). Every example
  program of the book *Programming the Pico Computer 3* runs, except those
  for the RGB512 and RGB1024 modes, which the PicoComputer 3 cannot show at
  640x480. RGB640 (640x480 in 256 colours) is shown with 16 colours chosen
  from the picture, exactly when it has 16 colours or fewer.
- **BBC Micro** (new in beta 2): the Colour Maximite 2's BBC Micro emulator
  (frank-micro, from B-em), the Model B and the Master 128, at the BBC's own
  50 frames a second with its sound, disc images and sideways ROMs. At
  720x576 each of the BBC's lines is shown twice, as on the Colour
  Maximite 2. Its 6502 and display code run from its SRAM part.
- **ScummVM** (new in beta 2): the SCUMM engine of the Colour Maximite 2's
  ScummVM (FRANK Quest), for the LucasArts adventures from Monkey Island to
  Full Throttle, with their speech, music and the USB mouse, at
  `OPTION RESOLUTION 640x480`. No games are included: copy the ones you own
  into folders of `B:/quest`, as its README describes. Day of the Tentacle
  (CD) has run on a PicoComputer 3 with its speech, music and the mouse.

## New in V8: USER modules (PicoMitePC3)

A USER module adds commands and functions of your own to MMBasic, written in
C and built with the same SDK (`build.py ... --user`). Copy the `.mod` file to
A: or B:, then

    USER LOAD "proforma"        ' .mod is added to a name without one
    USER HELLO "Peter"
    PRINT USER(ADD 2, 3.5)
    USER CLOSE

A module reads its part of the statement with the interpreter's own
routines, and its errors are MMBasic errors (`ON ERROR SKIP` catches them).
It goes with the heap: `RUN`, `NEW`, `LOAD`, `CHAIN`, `EDIT` and the file
manager drop it, and the next `USER` statement loads it again.
`tools/sdk/examples/user/proforma.c` is the one to start from.

## New in V8: FRAMEBUFFER WARPH and WARPV

On the VGA and HDMI builds, in MODE 2 to 5:

    FRAMEBUFFER WARPH from, to, xoff%() [,step] [,first] [,phase] [,edge] [,transparent] [,rows%()]
    FRAMEBUFFER WARPV from, to, yoff%() [,step] [,first] [,phase] [,edge] [,transparent]

copy framebuffer `from` to `to` with each row (WARPH) or column (WARPV)
moved by its own offset, read from a one-dimensional integer array: what
the arcade hardware called row scroll (or line scroll) and column scroll.

- Each element covers a band of `step` rows or columns, from row or column
  `first`.
- Band b uses element (b + `phase`) MOD n, so a table of offsets moves along
  just by counting `phase` up.
- The picture wraps round unless `edge` says otherwise: -1 leaves the
  uncovered pixels as they were, and a colour fills them.
- Pixels of the `transparent` colour are not copied, so warps can be layered.
- `rows%()` picks the source rows of each band, which gives a road its hills,
  a lake its reflection and a track its kerb stripes.

The offsets are usually made with the MATH commands (`MATH SCALE`,
`MATH ADD`, `MATH C_ADD`, `MATH CLAMP`), so a frame needs no loop over the
lines. On a PicoComputer 3 at 315 MHz a whole 320x240 MODE 2 screen takes
0.9 ms with WARPH, and 1.6 to 4.7 ms with WARPV.

The User Manual describes the commands under FRAMEBUFFER. The Game
Development Guide's new section *Raster Effects* has worked examples: a
parallax landscape, a lake that reflects the scene, a road with curves,
hills and kerbs, and a picture that melts away. `Bas/warp_demo.bas` in the
firmware's repository shows them all at the full frame rate (an RP2350, in
MODE 2 at 320x240).

## New in V8.00.00b2: PLAY MODSAMPLE for continuous sounds

    PLAY MODSAMPLE sampleno, channel [, volume] [, samplerate] [, pan] [, LOOP]
    PLAY MODSAMPLE CHANGE channel [, volume] [, samplerate] [, pan]
    PLAY MODSAMPLE STOP [channel]

A sample of the MOD file that is playing can now be a continuous sound
whose pitch, volume and place between the speakers follow the game, as on
the Colour Maximite 2:

- `samplerate` is new: 150 to 500000, default 16000 (the rate every
  sample played at before). It sets the pitch, and the length with it:
  twice the rate is an octave higher and half as long.
- `pan` is new: -100 plays the sample in the left speaker only, 100 in the
  right only, and 0 (the default, as every sample played before) in both
  at the full volume. Between them the far speaker is turned down in
  proportion, a simple balance: -50 is the full volume on the left and
  half on the right. The effects are now mixed after the music's stereo
  separation, so that a pan is exact; a centred effect is as loud as it
  was.
- With `LOOP` the sample plays until it is stopped: to the end of its own
  loop in the MOD file and back to the loop's start, as the MOD player
  plays it, or the whole sample over and over if it has no loop. `LOOP`
  ends the list after any argument from the channel on, so
  `PLAY MODSAMPLE 5, 1, LOOP` plays sample 5 on channel 1, looped.
- `CHANGE` changes the volume, the rate or the pan (any of them: an
  argument left empty is kept) of the sample playing on a channel, where
  it is, without starting it again: an engine that follows its revs, or
  the Doppler effect of a car going by from one side to the other. A
  channel that has stopped is left alone.
- `STOP` stops one channel, or all four. With no MOD file playing it does
  nothing, and is no error.
- On the RP2350 builds a sample is heard 12 to 23 ms after the command,
  not 46 to 93 ms: the MOD file is mixed 512 sample frames at a time, as
  the RP2040 builds always have (still 2048 with `OPTION AUDIO BLUETOOTH`,
  whose output takes up to 1024 frames at a time, and with a VS1053). A
  single statement that runs
  for longer than about 12 ms without the routine checks can now make the
  music repeat a short piece.
- The sample number is 1 to 31. A MOD file has 31 samples, and 32 was
  accepted but read past the end of the player's tables.

`Bas/doppler/doppler.bas` in the firmware's repository shows them: with
`doppler.mod`, a short tune made for it, playing, it moves an engine
between the speakers, then passes an engine and a car horn by at three
speeds, left to right and right to left.

## New in V8.00.00b3: SQL databases (PicoMitePC3 and PicoMiteHDMIUSB)

    SQL OPEN file$ [, poolKB [, cacheKB]]      SQL CLOSE [QUERY]
    SQL EXEC sql$ [, value ...]                SQL QUERY sql$ [, value ...]
    SQL CREATE type [, table$]                 SQL INSERT var [, table$]
    SQL(NEXT)  SQL(n)  SQL(FETCH var)  SQL(SELECT sql$, array() [, value ...])
    SQL(COLUMNS)  SQL(NAME n)  SQL(ISNULL n)  SQL(TYPE n)  SQL(ROWS)  SQL(ID)
    SQL(LONGSTRING n, a%())

A BASIC program can keep its data in an SQL database: SQLite, the same
engine as on the Colour Maximite 2, with the same commands, so a program
that uses them runs on either, and a database file can be read by SQLite on
a PC.

- The database is a file on B: (the SD card) or C: (a USB drive). A: is
  refused ("SQL: a database must be on B: or C:"): its flash file system
  would rewrite most of a database at each commit.
- SQLite is a module, `sql.mod`, in the `sql` folder of the programs zip:
  copy the folder to A: or B:. `SQL OPEN` loads it from `A:/sql/sql.mod` or
  else `B:/sql/sql.mod`, and `SQL CLOSE`, `RUN`, `NEW` and `CHAIN` unload it.
  While a database is open it takes about 2.6 MB of the heap in the PSRAM,
  and a stack of its own of 16 KB.
- Values go into statements as parameters (`?`, and `:name` from a
  structure); rows come out a value at a time (`SQL(n)`), into arrays
  (`SQL(SELECT`) or into structures (`SQL(FETCH`); `SQL CREATE` and
  `SQL INSERT` map a `TYPE` onto a table. Text longer than 255 characters
  goes in and out as a LONGSTRING.
- Ctrl-C stops a long statement with the error "SQL: stopped by Ctrl-C",
  which `ON ERROR` can catch.
- `SQL_Databases.pdf` describes it all, with an example; homa's mmsql 1.2,
  an interactive SQLite manager for the CMM2, passes its 51 tests on a
  PicoComputer 3 (with a few lines changed for the PicoMite).

## New in V8.00.00b3: FRAMEBUFFER AFFINE and SPAN

On the VGA and HDMI builds, in MODE 2 to 5:

    FRAMEBUFFER AFFINE from, to, u%(), v%(), du%(), dv%() [,step] [,first] [,left] [,width] [,edge] [,transparent]
    FRAMEBUFFER SPAN from, to, left%(), right%() [,step] [,first] [,fill] [,transparent] [,outside]

- `AFFINE` reads each row of `to` from `from` along a straight line of any
  direction and length. Where its first pixel comes from, and the step from
  one pixel to the next, are in integer arrays, in 1/65536 of a pixel, an
  element for each band of `step` rows. One element turns and zooms a whole
  picture; an element for each row draws a floor in perspective, what the
  consoles of the 1990s called Mode 7; `left` and `width` limit it to a
  window, which turns a sprite. Its `edge` and `transparent` are WARPH's.
- `SPAN` copies, on each row, only the columns from the row's own left entry
  to its right one, for an iris, a spotlight or a wipe of any shape. `fill`
  colours the rest of the row, and `outside` copies the outside instead.

The tables are made with the MATH commands, as WARPH's are. On a
PicoComputer 3 at 315 MHz a whole 320x240 MODE 2 screen takes 4.8 ms with
`AFFINE` at any angle and size, a floor 3.4 ms, and a `SPAN` 0.2 to 0.4 ms.
In MODE 3, 4 and 5 the PicoComputer 3's framebuffers are in its PSRAM, and an
`AFFINE` that shrinks the picture or turns it far is several times slower.

The User Manual describes both under FRAMEBUFFER, and the Game Development
Guide has two new sections of worked examples. `Bas/warp_demo.bas` has four
new scenes: a playing card turned by three shears that comes and goes as a
mosaic, a map turned and zoomed, the same map as the ground seen from a car
going round its track, and a torch in the dark and an iris.

## New in V8.00.00b4: threads

Every build can now run SUBs as threads alongside the main program, as the
Colour Maximite 2 does from its 8.00.00b5: a clock in the corner of the
screen, a tune played note by note, a sensor read every second, a long
calculation while the screen stays alive. Each thread has its own loops,
`LOCAL` variables and `PAUSE`s. One runs at a time, and they take turns
between statements, 1 ms each by default.

```
Dim Integer beats
Sub Heart
  Do
    Inc beats
    Pause 500             ' the rest of the program runs meanwhile
  Loop
End Sub
Thread Start 1, Heart
```

- `THREAD START n [SIZE kb] [LOCALS m] [NEST d] [PRIORITY p], sub [,
  argument ...]` starts thread n (1 to 8). `THREAD EXIT`, `STOP`, `YIELD`,
  `PAUSE`, `RESUME`, `SLICE`, `PRIORITY`, `SIZE`, `LOCK`, `UNLOCK`, `HOLD`,
  `RELEASE`, `WAIT`, `WAIT FLAG` and `LIST` manage them, and
  `MM.INFO(THREAD)`, `MM.INFO(THREAD n)` and `MM.INFO(THREAD LOCK k)` report
  on them.
- A thread that waits lets the others run: `PAUSE`, `INPUT`, the frame waits
  on the VGA and HDMI builds, `SYNC`, `TEMPR(`, `THREAD LOCK` and `WAIT`,
  and the network's and Bluetooth's commands. An interrupt runs in the
  thread that set it up.
- A thread of the defaults takes about 10.5 KB of the heap on a PicoMitePC3
  (its 8 KB stack and its record). A program that starts no thread runs as
  before, within a percent of its old speed.
- An error in any thread stops the program and every thread. It is shown as
  `[#3:40] x = a(i)` and then the message, and each thread's line and state
  is listed after it; `CONTINUE` is then refused.

The User Manual lists the commands and has an overview in its new Appendix
N. The MMBasic Threads User Manual (`MMBasic_Threads_User_Manual.pdf`, in
the supplementary manuals zip) describes them in detail, with worked
programs, for the Colour Maximite 2 and the PicoMite together.

## Changed in V8.00.00b4

- In a running program the listings that stop with PRESS ANY KEY at every
  screen full are now "Invalid in a program": `LIST` and `LIST file$`,
  `FLASH LIST n` and `RAM LIST n` without `ALL`, `LIBRARY LIST` without
  `ALL`, and `LIST VARIABLES`, `LIST COMMANDS`, `LIST FUNCTIONS` and `LIST
  PINS` without `ALL`. Use the `ALL` forms in a program. At the command
  prompt everything pages as before.
- `LIST VARIABLES ALL`, `LIST COMMANDS ALL`, `LIST FUNCTIONS ALL` and `LIST
  PINS ALL` are new: they list everything without pausing. `LIST VARIABLES
  s%()` and `LIST PINS #n` never paused and are unchanged.
- The MMBasic heap is 4 KB smaller on the PicoMiteRP2350USB, PicoMiteHDMI,
  PicoMiteHDMIUSB, PicoMiteHDMIWEB and PicoMitePC3 (104 KB of SRAM on the
  PC3), to leave the processor's stack the room it needs now that threads
  are in. The largest program is unchanged.

## New since V8.00.00b4

- A CSUB can let the program's other threads run. A CSUB is a single
  statement, and the threads take turns between statements, so until now
  every other thread waited until it returned. `CSubYield`, at offset 0x1C0
  in the CSUB call table (`PicoCFunctions.h` v2.4.0), gives them their turns
  and returns when this thread's comes round; mmb2csub calls it for a
  `THREAD YIELD` in a routine that it converts. Each of their turns is one
  time slice, so a thread with a burst of work to do while a CSUB runs puts
  `THREAD HOLD` and `THREAD RELEASE` round it.
- `Bas/threads/chess`: TSCP chess (Tom Kerrigan's, converted to MMBasic by
  Ceptimus and others) with a chess clock that runs as a thread. The
  computer's search is a CSUB with a `THREAD YIELD` every 128 nodes, so the
  clock keeps running while the computer thinks. The board is now redrawn
  after a capture of a like piece (pawn takes pawn), which used to leave the
  captured piece on show.

## Fixed since V8.00.00b4

- Threads (#167): `THREAD EXIT`, or `THREAD STOP` of its own thread, in an
  interrupt routine ended the thread with the routine still marked as
  running, and no thread switch or interrupt came again for the rest of the
  run, the main program's included. Both are now "Invalid in an interrupt
  routine": a routine ends its thread by setting a flag that the thread's
  loop tests. And a thread picked out of turn (for its interrupt routine, or
  as its `PAUSE` ended) restarted the turns after itself, so with a fast
  `SETTICK` in one thread beside busy ones the main program's `PAUSE` could
  never end. The turns now go round in order whatever is picked out of turn,
  and the thread whose turn a routine cut has the rest of it when the
  routine ends.
- `LGETSTR$` of 255 characters wrote a zero one byte past the end of its
  string, which could damage whatever was next in memory (#162).
- `WEB CONNECT` with no arguments rejoined the network only after a failed
  join, not when the link had simply gone down; it now rejoins whenever the
  board is not joined (#163).
- Threads: `FILES` in a thread (`EXECUTE "files"`, for example) crashed the
  board with a hard fault. In a program, `FILES` empties the heap for its
  list, and a thread's stack is in the heap. `FILES` is now "Not allowed in
  a thread", as `LIST` is; the main program can still use it while threads
  run. `RAM FILE LOAD` clears the runtime as it loads, which ended every
  thread (in a thread, after erasing the slot); it is now "Not allowed while
  a thread is running", as `SAVE CONTEXT` is.

## Fixed in V8.00.00b4

- RTC (#164): an RTC whose backup battery has failed starts again in 2000,
  and with `OPTION RTC AUTO ENABLE` that time was taken at every boot, and
  by the daily read over a good time set by `DATE$` and `TIME$` or `WEB
  NTP`. A time before 2018, or one the RTC itself marks as lost, is no
  longer taken: the boot says "RTC time not set", and `RTC GETTIME` gives
  that error.
- `WEB TRANSMIT FILE` (#165): a large file went at about half a second for
  each 1,460-byte chunk. It now goes as fast as the connection takes it.
- WebMite TLS client (#166): a connection that the server closed before it
  had sent anything (as a server does with an idle connection) left freed
  memory in use, and the next close could crash the board. A request on it
  now gives "No connection".
- `LIBRARY LOAD` as a program's first statement emptied `MM.CMDLINE$`; it
  now keeps it. In a program started by `CHAIN`, `FLASH CHAIN` or `RAM
  CHAIN` it wiped the variables that the chain had kept, and it is now the
  error "Invalid in a chained program".
- `RAM RUN`, `RAM CHAIN`, `FLASH RUN`, `FLASH CHAIN` and `FLASH LIST n` of
  an empty slot left the empty slot as the program in memory, so after the
  error `LIST` and `RUN` had the empty slot rather than the program that had
  been loaded. The program in memory now stays.
- `LOAD PNG` (RP2350 builds) uses 5.4 KB less stack: its decoder's tables
  are in its own heap block.

## Fixed in V8.00.00b3

- A program running the web server (`WEB TCP INTERRUPT`) skipped every
  error of its own after its first request, with no message: `MM.ERRNO` was
  set and the program ran on. It happened unless `ON ERROR SKIP` had been
  used since the board was reset, and a request also replaced a program's own
  `ON ERROR SKIP`, `IGNORE` or `RESTART`. Errors now stop the program as they
  should. A request that arrives when there is no memory for it is now held
  back and taken again a moment later, rather than raising an error inside the
  network code.
- `BLIT RESIZE` put the edges of its blocks a pixel late when the scale was
  not a power of two (5, 10 or 20 times, say): its step from one pixel to the
  next was rounded down, and the error added up across the rectangle. It now
  steps exactly: pixel k of the destination shows pixel k * sw \ dw of the
  source.
- `FORMAT$` gives what C's printf gives, to any precision: `%.17g` was
  exact to only about 15 digits (-2.5e-10 came out as
  -2.4999999999999973e-10, so a value written and read back changed), and an
  infinity came out as `INFe-2147483648`; it is now `inf` (`INF` for `%E`
  and `%G`), as `nan` is.

## Fixed in V8

- On every display, a shape that runs off an edge of the screen no longer
  draws a line of pixels along that edge. A `BOX` wholly off the screen, and
  each row of a filled `TRIANGLE` or `CIRCLE` that lay beyond the left edge,
  was drawn as a pixel in column 0 (and likewise at the other edges), because
  each display's rectangle drawing clamped the two ends of a line apart. All
  of them now clip through one routine. On the memory-buffered LCD panels a
  `PIXEL` off the screen is no longer put on its edge.

## Documentation

- The User Manual and the Game Development Guide describe `FRAMEBUFFER
  WARPH` and `WARPV`, and the BLIT, SPRITE and TILEMAP manuals point to
  them.
- The User Manual describes `PLAY MODSAMPLE`'s rate, pan and `LOOP`, and
  has entries of their own for `PLAY MODSAMPLE CHANGE` and `PLAY MODSAMPLE
  STOP`; `PLAY MODFILE`'s entry gives the size of its buffers. The Game
  Development Guide's MODSAMPLE section shows continuous sounds: an engine
  that follows its revs and the Doppler effect of a car going by from one
  speaker to the other.
- New in b3: the User Manual and the Game Development Guide describe
  `FRAMEBUFFER AFFINE` and `SPAN`; the guide also shows a mosaic made with
  `BLIT RESIZE` and `WARPH`.
- New in b3: `SQL_Databases.pdf`, a guide to the SQL command and `SQL(`
  (from the Colour Maximite 2's chapter, with the PicoMite's drives, memory
  and timings), and entries for `SQL` and `SQL(` in the User Manual.
- New in b4: the User Manual has entries for the `THREAD` commands and
  `MM.INFO(THREAD)`, an overview of threads in its new Appendix N, and the
  `ALL` forms of the listings. `MMBasic_Threads_User_Manual.pdf` joins the
  supplementary manuals.
- New since b4: the User Manual's `THREAD YIELD` entry and Appendix N, the
  mmb2csub guide and the CallTable appendix of `armcfgen.pdf` describe
  `CSubYield`. That appendix also listed `ExtCurrentConfig` at 0x1C0, a slot
  that no released firmware had it in: it is at 0x48. Appendix N's list of
  what a thread cannot do now includes `FILES` and `RAM FILE LOAD`.
- The SPRITE manual and the Game Development Guide said that a fill colour
  of -2 makes `SPRITE SCROLL` wrap the picture round. `SPRITE SCROLL` has
  never accepted -2: to wrap, leave the fill colour out.
