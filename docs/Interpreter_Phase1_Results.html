Phase 1 results · PicoMite MMBasic interpreter · 24 September 2026

Interpreter Phase 1 Results

The characterisation that decides the real changes. It covers overhead against native code on two more kernels, where the time goes in six workloads including three games, and what calls, name lookups, built-ins, graphics commands, interrupts, heap occupancy and block structure each cost on both chips. It ends with the order the evidence gives Route A.

Branch development at 0096659 (the PC sampler plus the five fixes from main). PC3: HDMIUSB, 378 MHz, configured as a Pico Computer 3 with 6 MB PSRAM. RP2040: PicoMiteVGA at 315 MHz. MicroPython: Peter's port on the PC2 (the same RP2350B) at 378 MHz. Every figure was measured on a board today. All figures below were checked against the raw outputs by independent reviewers.

1 · Summary

Summary

  • Overhead is higher in two compute kernels than in the anchor. Measured against their own CSUB twins:
    • julia kernel: 97% interpreter overhead on the PC3 and 95% on the RP2040.
    • Exile player routine: 99.4% on the PC3. It takes 9.36 ms a tick interpreted against 0.056 ms as a CSUB, about 170 times faster.
    • The anchor, for comparison: 81-87%.
  • Three buckets dominate every workload. Name lookup takes 16-34% of the samples on all six PC3 workloads (knivd, julia, a 3D frame, Exile, Elite, Prince of Pico), the evaluator 13-35%, and walking argument text 8-20%. Together they are 53-78%. The firmware's own drawing is 3-6% of a game frame.
  • Calls are not the biggest cost; H3's 20% threshold is borderline at most. Call machinery proper is 3-6% of the game samples. Counting the related samples as well (all of makeargs, zeroing and allocation) gives about 13-19%, with only Prince of Pico reaching the 20% that would move S3 ahead of symbols, and then only when FindSubFun is counted too.
    • Argument splitting is the largest item inside a call: makeargs, which splits both the caller's arguments and the SUB's own parameter list on every call, is 21% of a call-heavy loop.
    • Per LOCAL and per parameter, on the RP2350: each LOCAL scalar costs about 1,000 cycles (2,500-3,400 on the RP2040). Each AS INTEGER parameter costs about 860 cycles more than a % one.
    • A sefunc-shaped call: its 7 LOCAL arrays and 10 LOCAL scalars make it 3.6x (RP2350) to 4.2x (RP2040) slower than the same body on globals.
  • An armed SETTICK adds about 460 cycles to every statement on the RP2350 (630 on the RP2040), even if it never fires. That is 20-25% of a short statement like a%=a%+1, and about 14% of the Exile kernel's average statement. Any program with an interrupt armed pays it; none of the three measured games arms one.
  • The allocator's search depends on how full the heap is (H9 confirmed).
    • Top occupied: a program that DIMs big arrays first leaves the top of the heap occupied. Every LOCAL string or array allocation then scans past those pages: such a call gets 2.35-2.6x slower on the PC3 (+6,400 cycles, about 13 cycles a page) and 1.5x on the RP2040.
    • Bottom occupied: MID$ and STR$ temporaries pay about the same. Concatenation does not.
  • The RP2040 has two linear searches that the RP2350 does not.
    • Labels: a GOSUB to a label about 1,600 lines from the top costs 67x a near one.
    • The SUB table: with 200 SUBs defined, every array reference costs 32% more and every call about 2,100 cycles more (+47% on an empty SUB call, +21% on a FUNCTION call).
  • Recursion slows name lookup. At depth 10, a LOCAL read costs 46-48% more; globals and CONSTs read from the SUB slow down too.
  • Graphics commands are interpreter-bound.
    • Argument form: PIXEL, LINE, BOX and CIRCLE cost 1.37-1.52x as much with variable arguments as with literals on the RP2350, and 1.41-1.97x on the RP2040.
    • A PIXEL loop: 97% of it is interpretation.
    • Against MicroPython on the same chip: PIXEL and LINE are 2.7-3.3x slower at top level and 3.2-5.2x inside a SUB; BOX and CIRCLE 1.4-2.3x (BOX with variables inside a SUB 3.1x). Big fills are equal, so the C drawing matches. Long lines are the exception: MMBasic's C line routine is 2.6x slower than MicroPython's framebuf.
  • Smaller costs:
    • SELECT: about 400 cycles for each arm it tests, and even a first-arm match scans every remaining arm. A 30-arm SELECT costs 10,800-22,600 cycles on the RP2350.
    • FOR and DO entry: the scan of the body costs 210-270 cycles per body line.
    • REM and DATA lines: about 3x a ' comment line.
    • USB host polling: 1-3.5% on HDMIUSB.
  • A latent RP2040 bug was found and fixed on main (48b4680): variables could take a SUB's name without error (section 11).

  • Per parameter (RP2350). About 1,840 cycles for each % parameter, and 860 more if it is declared AS INTEGER. BYVAL adds about 1,080. Literal arguments are about 800 cycles a call cheaper than variables.
  • Per LOCAL. About 1,000-1,140 cycles for each LOCAL scalar on the RP2350, and 2,500-3,400 on the RP2040. A single LOCAL la(3) costs about 2,700 cycles on an empty heap. The full heap of section 8 adds its search on top.
  • The sefunc-shaped call costs 3.6x (RP2350) and 4.2x (RP2040) a SUB doing the same work on global arrays with no LOCALs. The 7 LOCAL arrays account for most of that and the 10 LOCAL scalars for the rest. The anchor makes only 474 such calls, about 50 ms of its 12 s; it matters in code that calls LOCAL-array SUBs every frame.
  • The split inside a call. A loop of calls to a SUB with 6 AS INTEGER parameters and 4 LOCALs was sampled.
    • The largest items: findvar 25%, makeargs 21%, DefinedSubFun 8.5%, strchr 5.5%, skipvar 5.1%, memset 4.7%, and checkstring 4.5% (the AS INTEGER parse).
    • What makeargs covers: it splits both the caller's argument list and the SUB's own definition line on every call.
    • The definition-side part: makeargs plus the type parse come to about 26%, most of it on the definition side, which call descriptors (S3b) would do once. The first H3 test, definition side at least 25% of call cost, is roughly met.
  • Calls are a modest share of real code.
    • The call machinery bucket: 6.4% of the Exile kernel, 2.8% of Elite and 5.0% of Prince of Pico.
    • Counting related samples: adding all of makeargs (which also serves commands), memset and GetMemory gives about 15%, 13% and 19%. That leaves out FindSubFun (with it Prince of Pico reaches about 20%) and findvar's creation of parameters and LOCALs.
    • The second H3 test is borderline. It needs calls at least 20% of the game samples. Exile and Elite are well under, and Prince of Pico sits at the threshold only when every related sample is counted. S3 therefore stays after the symbol work, which also removes much of a call's lookup cost.
Sampler caveat

The sampler's tables (16 bytes an entry) come from the heap. At 8,192 entries each table is 64 KB, under half the PC3's 150 KB SRAM heap, so both land in SRAM and every GetMemory in a profiled run scans past them. In the sampled 7-LOCAL-array loop GetMemory took 64% of the samples: section 8's effect caught live. The game profiles used 16,384 entries, which go to PSRAM and avoid it. The sampler should allocate outside the SRAM heap, and should stop before END prints its report.

For drawing commands the arguments do matter.

  • Variable against literal: the variable form costs 1.37-1.52x the literal form on the RP2350 and 1.41-1.97x on the RP2040. H4's 1.5x test is met by PIXEL on both chips and by LINE and BOX on the RP2040 only. What the ratio prices is a name lookup against a short integer parse.

7 · Interrupts and the per-statement tail (H5)

  • Arming one SETTICK adds about 460 cycles to every statement on the RP2350 and about 630 on the RP2040, whether or not it fires. That is +25% and +21% on a%=a%+1, or +42% and +36% on the whole a%=a%+1/NEXT pass.
    • The cost is in checking, not firing. Letting the tick fire every millisecond adds only about 110-120 cycles more per pass, about 60 a statement.
    • The likely fix: an interrupt-pending word set by the ISRs (S1c) should remove most of it for SETTICK. That is still to be measured. Polled sources, such as COM levels and pin interrupts, need converting as well.
  • OPTION NOCHECK ON removes about 130 cycles (RP2350) and 150 (RP2040) of checks per statement with nothing armed, 7% and 5% of a%=a%+1. That is consistent with Phase 0's tail estimate.
  • On HDMIWEB, Phase 0 priced ProcessWeb at about 10% of the anchor. Peter's constraint for S1a: WEB SCAN runs with no WiFi connected and needs ProcessWeb while it executes. A cadence change therefore cannot depend on a live connection, and any command that waits on the network must still run ProcessWeb itself.
8 · Heap occupancy (H9)

The allocator's page search

There are two searches, page by page through the SRAM heap (core/Memory.c):

  • GetMemory (variables, LOCAL strings and arrays, DIM) searches from the top down.
  • GetSystemMemory (temporary strings and the like) searches from the bottom up.

So the same statements were timed in three heap states:

  • Nearly empty.
  • Top 80% occupied: big arrays DIMmed first, the usual real-world state.
  • Bottom 80% occupied: only possible after the heap has once been full.

On the PC3 the fill was kept in SRAM, below the size that goes straight to PSRAM.

  • H9 is confirmed at 1.45-2.6x against a 1.15x threshold. The fix is local to Memory.c: keep a first-free hint for each search direction, so a search starts where free pages begin. It changes no format and costs a few bytes (new step S10).

  • Entering a FOR or DO scans its body. That costs 214 (FOR) and 266 (DO) cycles per body line on the RP2350, and about 250 on the RP2040. An inner FOR with a 30-line body pays about 6,400 cycles (RP2040 7,600) every time it is entered.
  • SELECT scans every arm up to END SELECT, even after a match. Each arm tested costs about 400 cycles on the RP2350 (740 on the RP2040), and a first-arm match still scans the remaining arms at about 300 cycles each. A 30-arm SELECT costs 10,800 cycles when the first arm matches and 22,600 when the last does (RP2040: 16,500 and 37,900). Partner tables (S7) make both nearly constant.
  • On the RP2040, a GOSUB to a label about 1,600 lines from the top of the program costs 241,000 cycles, 67x a near label (the label sits past a 1,000-line SUB). The RP2350 shows no difference. H8's RP2040 test of at least 2x is met many times over; S5b label symbols fix it.

The RP2040 bug found on the way, now fixed on main (48b4680, merged into development):

  • The bug. When findvar created a variable on the RP2040, its check for a SUB or FUNCTION of the same name stepped one byte into the 2-byte command token instead of over it. It never matched, so a variable could take a SUB's name without the error the RP2350 gives.
  • The fix and its proof. DIM foo beside SUB foo and LOCAL fn beside FUNCTION fn now raise "A sub/fun has the same name". Prefixes and extensions of a SUB's name are still accepted, and no corpus golden hits the error on either chip.
  • What it costs. The check now really compares names on every LOCAL created, which costs solar_eclipse.bas 1.0% on the RP2040 (32.66 s to 32.99 s). Replacing the scan with a binary search of the sorted SUB table, as FindSubFun does, would remove it; that could join S2.

Outside the interpreter, for Peter's list:

  • Long lines: MMBasic's C DrawLine takes 2.6x MicroPython's cycles on long lines at the same 4-bit packed format. MicroPython's own 8-bit mode is only 13% faster, so it is the line loop, not the format.
  • USB host polling: takes 1-3.5% on HDMIUSB.
  • TestStackOverflow: takes 2.1-2.6% of every RP2040 workload.
  • The sampler: its tables should come from outside the SRAM heap, and it should stop before END prints its report.

What stays for Route B: the evaluator core, 13-35% of the samples.

12 · Method and files

How it was measured

  • Builds.
    • PC3: HDMIUSB at 0096659. Its micro suite matched Phase 0 within 3% (most within 1%). The board missed its automatic PC3 configuration on the first boot (the GP27 detection) and was configured by hand.
    • RP2040: the timing runs were on the main build carrying the five fixes (b4ccc22). The profiles were on 0096659, after it turned out the main build had no sampler; that is also why two early RP2040 profiled runs printed [PERF] with no [PCS]. The two builds differ only by the sampler code, within the 1% placement floor.
  • Micro tests time N passes of a loop holding one statement and subtract the empty loop, as in Phase 0. Timing runs never had profiling on; profiled runs are used only for shares.
  • Game workloads are modified copies, never the originals. They are headless, run a fixed number of frames with fixed seeds, and check their end state. After review, the Exile harness's calibration pass was corrected and the three games were rerun. That moved the Exile CSUB kernel from 0.046 to 0.056 ms a tick, and the figures above are from the rerun. The Prince of Pico data is Peter's local conversion and is not in the repository.
  • Files. The programs and drivers are in Bas/bench/phase1:
    • gen_micro2.py (bench_micro2, bench_subs0/200), bench_heap.bas, bench_gfx.bas / bench_gfx.py, calls_pcs_*.bas;
    • macros/: knivd_fixed, the julia pair, gfx_frames and runmacro.py;
    • games/: the builder make_games.py and gamebench.py. It builds the game copies from the repository, so no game data is stored.
    The raw outputs and phase1_data.json are in Bas/bench/results/phase1.
